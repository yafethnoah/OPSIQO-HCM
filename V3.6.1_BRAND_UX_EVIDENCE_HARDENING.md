# OPSIQO HCM v3.6.1 — Full Brand, UX & Evidence Hardening

## Purpose
This hardening pass addresses the runtime defects observed during the frame-by-frame UI review while preserving the reviewed dependency graph and existing human-approval/security boundaries. It also applies the supplied OPSIQO wordmark and glowing Q icon as the production brand system across the platform.

## Brand system
- Uses the supplied OPSIQO full composition and Q icon as the source identity.
- Adds optimized runtime assets under `public/brand/` plus Next-native `icon.png`, `apple-icon.png` and favicon assets.
- Applies jet-black/deep-navy surfaces, steel text, electric-blue and cyan-glow accents across shell, cards, forms, tables, tabs, public/auth surfaces and state components.
- Rebrands sign-in, first-organization setup and prehire surfaces so the product identity is consistent before and after authentication.
- Adds independent sidebar/content scrolling, grouped/collapsible navigation, active-route treatment and compact sidebar mode.
- Retains responsive/mobile layouts, reduced-motion support and high-contrast accommodations.

## Evidence truthfulness
The Enterprise HCM Index and direct governance workspaces now separate **evidence sufficiency** from readiness/risk.
- Domains below the minimum evidence threshold are `not_assessed` rather than healthy or critical by default.
- Unassessed domains are excluded from the weighted enterprise readiness score and risk penalties.
- The Command Center reports evidence coverage and assessed-domain count explicitly.
- Integration, Identity, Security Operations and Platform Reliability withhold readiness percentages when evidence is insufficient.
- Privacy, Audit & Assurance, Workforce Resilience, Human Capital Strategy and Organization Design now apply the same evidence threshold directly on their overview pages.
- Organization Design also withholds structural-health/span/layer conclusions until a governed structure snapshot exists.
- Assurance withholds evidence/test-effectiveness percentages when the supporting record populations do not exist.
- Lifecycle cards use `N/A` rather than unsupported readiness values.
- AI evidence retrieval communicates insufficient evidence and does not repeat unsupported readiness conclusions.
- Zero incidents/events are not treated as proof of control effectiveness.
- Compliance no longer reports 100% when there are no applicable requirements; it returns `Not assessed` with explicit evidence coverage.

## Loading and empty states
- People and Audit Trail distinguish `loading`, `loaded_empty`, and `loaded_with_data` states, eliminating the false empty-state flashes seen in the runtime recording.
- Privacy, Assurance, Org Design, HR Diagnostic, Lifecycle, Regulatory Change and Security Posture now use the shared loading-state treatment instead of raw cards/text.
- Major workspaces reload when the active organization changes.
- Audit export uses the auth-restored download boundary instead of directly reading Firebase `currentUser` during hydration.

## Authentication and organization context
- Firebase API calls wait for auth restoration before treating `currentUser === null` as a sign-out.
- The organization switcher re-evaluates memberships when Firebase auth state changes.
- First-organization setup is available at `/setup` through the guarded bootstrap flow already added to v3.6.1.
- Sign-in and setup surfaces now share the OPSIQO branded authentication shell.

## Firestore deployment repair
- Removes the redundant single-field composite index for `analyticsSnapshots.snapshotDate` that Firestore rejects with HTTP 400 because automatic single-field indexing already covers it.
- No other single-field `COLLECTION` composite indexes remain in `firestore.indexes.json`.

## Dependency and security boundaries
- `package.json` is unchanged.
- The reviewed `package-lock.json` is unchanged.
- Firestore security rules are unchanged.
- Existing independent approvals, AI consequential-decision prohibitions, organization isolation and server-only collection boundaries remain intact.
- Readiness is an operational governance indicator, never a legal opinion or certification.
